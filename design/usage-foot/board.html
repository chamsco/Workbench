1. Where usage lives

Studied first: today the user row shows "62% left" as small grey text under your name, and Usage is the fifth button in the apps row, where it swaps the sidebar list. Both directions take Usage out of the apps row.

A · Ring beside your name Pick

The destinations board's pattern: one ring with the lowest share left, as its own button in the user row. Hovering it shows every account; clicking opens the Usage page in the card. Your name still opens subscriptions.

B · Meter row above your name

A labelled row on top of the user row with one bar per account. Readable without hovering, but it costs 34px of the list on every app and turns into a row of slivers past three accounts.

Decision: A. It is the layout you pointed at (the destinations board) and costs no height, so the stream list keeps its room. Comprehension: the ring answers "am I about to run out" from the worst account, and the percentage beside it is in words, not only colour; it turns amber under 20%. Discoverability: the ring has a tooltip-sized hover card that names every account, so B's always-on labels are not needed. Interaction cost: one hover to see all accounts, one click to the page. Accessibility: the button's name reads "Usage, 38% left on Codex", and the hover card's rows are text. The risk: when no CLI reports a limit the ring would be empty, so it shows a dash and the card says which accounts were found but have no limits yet.
