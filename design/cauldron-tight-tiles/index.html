Tight tiles: the Zuse-inspired look for Cauldron

From your 13 screenshots I took the small corner radius, the 4px gaps between panels, the widget sizes, the six-step onboarding and the logo loader. The right split is always a support panel for the open thread. Nothing here is built. Zuse's logo, its space-invaders backdrop, its dithered green buttons and its copy are not reused.

Round 4 sets the look: folded is one card with a notch and an inner rail of quick buttons, open is tight tiles with tabs on top of the support card and the same rail. Rounds 1 to 3 are folded away at the bottom.

Round 4 · the look

Folded: one card with a notch. Open: tight tiles.

When the right side is folded, the thread is one card. Its quick buttons stand in a rail at the card's right edge, and the card's top-right corner steps in as a notch around the window buttons. Opening any quick button splits off a support card 4px to the right. That card has tabs on top for what you opened, and the same rail of quick buttons on its right edge under the same notch. The rail and the notch never move. Only the card they belong to changes.

Rules. A rail icon opens its panel as a tab in the support card, or focuses it if it is already open. Opening the first one splits the card off. Closing the last tab folds the right side back into the one-card state. The support card only ever shows panels for this thread. The window buttons always sit on the grey inside the notch.
What this replaces. The grey drawer from rounds 2 and 3 is gone. Its five panels (Highlights, Patches as Changes, Git, Agent trace as Trace, Dev as Browser) are now tabs in the support card. The cost is that Highlights is no longer visible beside a thread at no width cost: you open it as a tab. If you want a glance-only strip back, it could sit at the top of the rail as a tooltip on hover.

Round 9 · small interactions

The + menu, open-in, scroll ticks and the context tab

These come from the Zuse close-ups you sent. All of them are drawn in the shell and shown here so you can judge them as part of the whole window.

Scroll ticks. On the right edge of each thread there is a column of short lines, one per response. The accent one is where you are. Hover shows that response's first words, and click jumps to it. Taller ticks mark responses with a diff or a question for you. It is a quick way to move in a long thread, and it sits clear of the right rail.
Context tab above the box. On a new thread, project, worktree and import chat sit in a tab that the box overlaps, with the GitHub link at the far right. It reads as part of the box, like the reference, instead of loose text above it.
Round 7 · theme

Painting behind everything, frosted frame, glass cards

Every window on this board now sits on a background, using the Harbour paintings from the old Tauri app (dusk in dark, day in light). The frame and sidebar are frosted: a strong blur under a tint, so the painting is only colour and light there. The thread and support cards are glass: a light blur under a thinner tint, so you can still make out the shapes, with the text on top kept readable. The composer box, terminal, diffs and browser viewport sit a step more opaque because that is where you read and type. The notch is a real cut in the card, so the frame shows through it. Use the Painting / Plain switch at the top to compare.

Layers, back to front. 1. The background slot, which takes the painting, the S1 shader or a GIF. 2. One frosted frame layer over the whole window (28px blur, 52% tint in light, 70% in dark so small text holds up). The sidebar, the gaps between cards, the strip around the window buttons and the Mac indent are all this same layer, so they match. 4. The cards, with a 10px blur and 58% tint. 5. Reading surfaces (composer 82%, terminal 70%, diff 70%).
What it costs in GPUI. GPUI has no backdrop blur, as found on 13 Nov. So frosted and glass are each a pre-blurred copy of the background, cropped per element and cached by size. A still painting costs almost nothing. A moving shader or GIF has to be re-blurred every frame: about 2ms a frame at quarter resolution from the Mac benchmark. So motion should only run on the new-thread screen, as decided for the shader.
Round 5 · macOS

Mac, with both sides folded

The Mac buttons sit top left, so there is no notch. The card runs to the right edge and the rail starts right under the header. With the sidebar folded, the card's header starts with the traffic lights, then the sidebar toggle and New thread, then the tabs. With the pointer at the left edge, the grey frame grows into an indent.

Hover rules. The indent appears after the pointer has rested about 150ms in the strip at the left edge, so passing through on the way to the lights doesn't trigger it. It is drawn over the card rather than pushing it, so nothing reflows. It hides 300ms after the pointer leaves. The sidebar button stays in the card header, and Cmd+B pins the full sidebar open.
What to watch. On Mac the left edge is also where you resize the window, so the hover strip sits 4px inside the edge. A trackpad user who never goes near the edge must still find the sidebar, which is why the toggle stays in the header next to the lights.
Measured, not guessed

What the reference actually does

A pixel scan of your Windows shots at 100% scaling shows the following: main and support cards have a 6px corner, they sit 4px apart on a #1e1e1e frame, and each card is #151515 with a 1px #212121 hairline. The sidebar has no card and sits on the frame. Tabs live inside each card's header at 28px tall, and the composer box rounds at about 14px. Our design-system dark surfaces (#1d1d1d shell, #151515 panel) already match almost exactly, so the change is geometry, not colour.

First run

Onboarding in six steps

This follows Zuse's sequence, with Cauldron's own content: Welcome, Agents, Machines, Import, Make it yours, Ready. Zuse asks you to sign in at step 3. Cauldron has no account to sign into, so step 3 connects machines instead, which uses the model server on :7422. The progress bar is segmented rather than continuous, and the background is the dot texture from earlier Cauldron frames.

Launch

Loader: the mark with a light thread

A thin thread of light runs around the outline of the Cauldron mark, over a faint copy of the outline, with "Opening Cauldron" underneath. In dark it is white, as you asked. In light, a white thread disappears, so it runs in ink. The mark is new because the app has no logo yet (the GPUI build draws an 18px purple circle). It is a simple cauldron outline, deliberately unlike Zuse's Z. With reduced motion turned on, the full outline shows without animating.

Earlier rounds 1 to 3 (superseded, kept for reference)
Your question

The drawer: a third part on the grey, or inside the second pane?

Two ways to bring back Highlights, Patches, Git, Agent trace and Dev.

Why R1. The two do different jobs. The drawer is for glancing: highlights, what changed, who is working, and whether the dev server is up. It is narrow, it stays open, and it costs the thread only 212px. The support card is for work: reading a full diff, clicking through the app, browsing files. That needs 420px or more and a card. R2 forces both into one card, so checking Highlights would hide the Browser you were using. R1 also keeps the grey-extension look you liked.
The rule that keeps R1 from crowding. Opening a split moves the drawer's five icons into the split's own header, left of its tabs, and its panels open inside the card. Closing the split puts the drawer back on the grey. Patches and Dev each have "Open in split", which moves them from a glance to the full tool. A thread never has two expanded things on the right at once.
Round 3 · window buttons

Keep minimise, maximise and close on one surface

With a split open, the buttons sat half on the folded rail (grey) and half on the support card. Here are three fixes. Each keeps all three buttons wholly on the grey or wholly in a card, in every state.

Why W2. It costs no height and adds no new shape, and it gives the right side the tab system you described. The buttons never move, only the surface under them changes, and that surface is always whole. The one thing to watch is that the split header now carries five drawer icons, its tabs and the buttons. At 420px that fits two tabs, and a third collapses to "+1". W1 is the fallback if you'd rather the buttons never sit inside a card.
Two directions

Where the support panel lives

Both use the measured radii. They differ in whether the support panel is its own tile or a strip inside the thread's card.

Why A. It is the only one of the two that shows the 4px gaps you asked for, because a gap needs two objects. As a separate card with labelled tabs, the support panel reads as a tool for this thread rather than part of the conversation. Switching between Changes and Browser is one click on a visible label, while B makes you remember six unlabelled icons. Each tile also scrolls and resizes on its own, so a long diff never moves the composer.
What A costs. It has more edges on screen, and three tiles plus the sidebar can look busy. I kept every tile the same fill with only a hairline, and no shadows. Below 1100px wide the support card can't hold 360px next to a usable thread, so it takes over the thread column (expand icon) instead of squeezing it. B stays calmer on small laptops, and that is its one real advantage.

Tabs sit inside the thread card's header, so the separate top bar is gone. Usage per subscription sits under the box, as on v2.
