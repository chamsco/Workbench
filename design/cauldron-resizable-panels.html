Resizable panels that learn your width

Charles: sidebar and panels resize, learn the width you prefer and snap to it, weighted for recency, and the snap unlocks for precise resizing. This is a working prototype at real pixel sizes. Drag the two handles.

My pick. A: one magnet at the recency-weighted peak of the widths you have settled on, with Alt (Option) or Shift held to move freely. Double-click a handle to return to your usual width. Arrow keys move a focused handle by 8px, Alt + arrow by 1px.

How I read the brief. "Learn" means it records the width you let go at, not the width the panel opens at. "Weighted for recency" means each resize counts half as much after 5 newer ones, so a changed habit takes over within about 6 resizes. "Snap unlocks" means holding Alt or Shift releases the magnet, and dragging more than the snap radius away does the same. Shift is there because many Linux window managers take Alt+drag for themselves.

1. Try it

Both panels start with a history. Drag the sidebar near 248px: it pulls in, locks, and lets go when you move away. Hold Alt or Shift and it never pulls. Then press "I now prefer something else", drag to a new width a few times and watch the peak move.

2. Two models

The toggle above switches between them with the same unlock.

A. Learned peakBuilds a density curve from your settled widths, each weighted by recency, and puts one magnet on its highest point. It snaps only when it has seen at least 2 resizes' worth of weight and the peak holds a third of it. The snap radius grows with confidence, 8 to 16px. With two habits it follows the one with more recent weight and keeps the other as a bump on the curve.
B. Last 3 widthsMagnets at the last three distinct widths you left the panel at. Easy to explain, no model. With varied widths it puts three magnets within reach and the drag feels sticky everywhere; it forgets a long-held habit after three odd resizes.

3. Decision card

PickA. Recency decay by resize count, not by time, so a preference survives a week away from the app.
ComprehensionWhile dragging, the handle turns purple and a label shows the width and "your usual" when it is locked. The chart here is for the prototype and Settings, not the app chrome.
Interaction costNo setup. The cost is one surprise: the first time the panel pulls toward a width. The tooltip on the handle says "Drag to resize, Alt or Shift for exact, double-click for your usual".
PrecisionAlt or Shift held lets you land on any pixel. Dragging more than the radius away leaves the magnet without a jump: inside 40% of the radius it locks, then eases out, so there is no pop at the edge.
Failure modesFeedback loop: a snapped release reinforces the same peak. Decay handles it, and leaving the zone needs a deliberate move. Single odd resize: ignored until the peak holds a third of the weight. Small windows: widths are clamped and not recorded when the window forces the clamp.
KeyboardHandles are focusable separators: arrows 8px, Alt + arrow 1px, Home and End go to the minimum and maximum.
Applies toLeft sidebar (min 200, max 360), right drawer on started streams (280 to 560), Agents drawer (168 to 280), Library note panel (220 to 420). Each panel has its own history. Settings, Appearance, Panels has "Reset learned widths".
Build notesThe model is a pure function (history in, snap target and radius out) with tests: peak position, continuity of the magnet at both radii, and a drift test (6 resizes at a new width move the peak). Persist the last 40 widths per panel in the app settings. GPUI drag handle with mouse capture; check that Alt and Shift are readable on mouse move.
